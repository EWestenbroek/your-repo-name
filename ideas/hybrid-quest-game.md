# Hybrid Quest Game

Status: idea

## Problem / Motivation

A video game that blends digital gameplay with real-world quests. The two halves are interdependent: progress needs both kinds of quests. But no single player has to do both. Players can specialize in the side they enjoy and trade with others who prefer the other side, or don't want to deal with a particular task. Doing both yourself gives the full experience.

## Rough Approach

- **One shared game, two kinds of quests:** digital quests (played in-game) and real-world quests (done out in the world). Progress depends on both kinds; neither side is a standalone game.
- **Specialize through other players:** a player can focus on one side by resolving quests *for* other players. A real-world-focused player handles real-world tasks for digital-focused players, and the other way round. This also works per task, not only per side: hand off any single quest you don't want to bother with.
- **Doing both yourself is the full game:** generalists experience everything first-hand. Specialists get the rest through other players.
- **Real-world quests center on scavenger hunts:** find places, objects or clues out in the world. A hunt can feed into the digital side, e.g. a found clue unlocks an in-game area or item.
- **Multiplayer is built into the core loop:** the hand-off mechanic makes the game social by design. Genre leaning: multiplayer RPG (not decided yet).

## Open Questions

- Multiplayer RPG, or something else? Co-op, guild/party-based, or an open marketplace?
- How are quests handed off? A quest board/marketplace, direct requests to friends, guild assignments, automatic matchmaking?
- What does the helper get in return? In-game currency, a share of the loot, reputation, items only obtainable this way?
- What does the requester give up by outsourcing a quest? Lower rewards, missing story beats, cost?
- What kind of scavenger hunts? Find a location (GPS), find a hidden code/tag (QR/NFC), photograph something specific, solve a riddle tied to a real place?
- Who creates the hunts? Game designers, procedural generation from map data, or players creating hunts for each other?
- How do hunts scale across cities vs. rural areas without feeling sparse?
- Any other real-world quest types besides scavenger hunts, or keep it focused?
- How is real-world completion verified, especially when done on someone else's behalf? GPS, QR codes/NFC tags, photos, peer review?
- How do you keep it balanced if most players prefer one side (e.g. too few people willing to do real-world quests)?
- Abuse and exploits: bots farming digital quests, faked real-world proof, real-money trading.
- Safety and accessibility of real-world quests (location, mobility, weather, privacy).
- Platform: mobile only, or PC/console paired with a mobile companion app?
